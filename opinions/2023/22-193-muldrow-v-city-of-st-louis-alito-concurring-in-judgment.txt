Justice Alito, concurring in the judgment.
I agree with the judgment in this case. Assuming with-
out deciding that all the facts mentioned by the Court are
relevant and properly presented, petitioner's transfer al-
tered the “terms” or “conditions” of her employment, 42
U. S. C. § 2000e–2(a)(1), and therefore she can prevail if she
can prove that she was transferred because of her sex.
I do not join the Court's unhelpful opinion. For decades,
dozens of lower court judges, with a wealth of experience
handling Title VII cases, have held that not every unwanted
employment experience affects an employee's “terms” or
“conditions” of employment. The lower courts have used
various verbal formulations to express this point, and the
Court, dubious about the words they had selected, granted
review to provide guidance. Now, after briefing and argu-
ment, that guidance is as follows: Title VII plaintiffs must
show that the event they challenge constituted a “harm” or
“injury,” but that the event need not be “significant” or “sub-
stantial.” See ante, at 354–355, 358.
I have no idea what this means, and I can just imagine
how this guidance will be greeted by lower court judges.
